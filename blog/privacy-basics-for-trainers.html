Three habits every trainer should teach on day one

4 min read · Published 17 September 2026

I'm pretty sure that you always get to see news about data breaches in daily basis. They usually start with a habit like reusing the same password for all the accounts whether it's their Google or Microsoft Account or the company bank account.A link clicked too quickly or an employee who's working from home leaving their laptop unlocked at a cafe table to go to the washroom quickly. From my experience, onboarding process is the best time that you can teach newbies about these things, because you're guranteed everyone's attention. The very best moment to install better defaults before bad ones set in.

Creating complex passwords and setting up 2FA should not just be a company policy

Many employees only take this into consideration if there's a random IT audit or someone from the IT department asking all employees to follow this policy, but the worse case is that even then, most employees don't understand the gravity of the situation and what's at stake here. Usually, the best way to teach something to the employees is to convince them that something is useful to them personally, outside of work, which is the same technique you can use in this situation. Teaching them about the importance of having complex passwords and setting up 2FA (or MFA) to their Google, Microsoft, or social media accounts in their personal lives should automatically enough them to convince that they have to apply the same to their work accounts as well. Speaking from experience!

Separate "locking your screen" from "being paranoid"

It's actually being paranoid :), but again, if you ever being a trainer or a teacher, you might understand how people are catching things from different ends. Here, framing locking the device screen when the employee is leaving their seat is obviously something that you should be highlighted as a security requirement to align with the company policies. Also, I suggest framing it as a basic courtesy, the same as not reading someone's messages over their shoulders and make it a norm rahter than make it a strict rule.

In practice: being paranoid is sometimes good!

Normalize saying "I'm not exactly sure this is legitimate".

This is one of the most underated sentence that I have seen in many valuable companies. They are really good employers who provide all the latest and greatest tech equipments to their employees, but most of the digital attacks happen unfortunately, due to a human error. It should be a normal practice that employees question about the legitimacy of emails/forms that they come across specially nowadays since deepfakes and phishing attacks which used to be time and resource consuming to be created are sooooooooooooooooooooooooooo common thanks to AI. Many companies annually lose hundreds of millions of dollars unfortunately due to these attacks which could be preventable easily by including these during the onboarding and occasional followups from time to time.

The baseline is that,

  • None of these require having a security background in tech
  • All of these topics take about 30 minutes to teach with many real life examples
  • The goal is to make it a habit among employees, not just a passed quiz

You will actually be doing a help to the society as well because many people suffer a lot by being a victim to these threats daily and at the end of the day, you have the peace in your mind thinking that you helped someone out there!
