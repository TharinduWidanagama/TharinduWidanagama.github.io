Please stop using AI to create Learning and Marketing materials

7 min read · Published 18 October 2026

When you go to LinkedIn nowadays (or just any other social media platform in that subject), it's just very difficult to identify the difference between the flyers/marketing materials shared by companies/corporations due to the heavy usage of AI Image Generation. We used to have creative marketing campaigns throughout the history which were very powerful and anyone could have easily guessed the brand or the company behind the advertisement. As an example, close your eyes for a second and think about a colour and then, see what brands come to your mind. When you think about the colour Red, maybe it's about Pepsi or Coca-Cola. Or if it's Green, maybe Starbucks, Carlsberg, Spotify or WhatsApp. What's the brand that comes to your mind when you think about shoes? The companies behind the famous brands that we see and use in our day-to-day lives are basically evidences of successful marketing and their unique thinking patterns.

We have seen marketing wars between companies (Coke vs Pepsi, Apple vs Microsoft - though GNU/Linux at the corner, laughing) and they were really REALLY entertaining and some of us were even on our toes counting days when they were going to be releasing the next advertisement. Sales boosted, social media numbers skyrocketed, and the brands were actually able to make some pretty long term positive feelings in consumers' minds. Now, fast forwarding to 2026, the creativity is gradually going downhill. We used to see 50 advertisements per week and now, we see 500 advertisements instead, but consumers are not happy. No one's paying attention to the flyers and companies and brands are left there hanging dry wondering why.

Also, if you're an Instructional Designer, a Trainer or maybe just someone who just needs a presentation, please don't use AI to quickly generate your work and I have witnessed how it immediately kills the engagement of the audience because they understand that it's AI generated and loses the trust even though the data are accurate. Specially, if you're conducting an onboarding, make sure you don't break that trust. One of the key reasons why we do an onboarding is to build TRUST. If you're a beginner to Learning & Development, Instructional Designing and Training industry, I'd suggest you to spend time to curate your craft which is your presentation. It might take hours to prepare your very first template, but keep practicing and you'll love it when others say that they loved your presentation.

So, let's talk about the elephant in the room now.

Yeah, AI Generated materials are fast, but it comes with a price. Unless, the user defines the colour palette or branding assets properly (although the chance is that still the free versions of AI models would generate it wrong), all the output materials look the same. The same faces, the same skin colour tones, the same dull colours that don't complement each other, the same assets and then the companies and the brands are complaining their social media post has less engagement and people are not interacting with it. It's basic human psychology. Let's take McDonald's as an example here. Throughout many decades, McDonald's has been able to generate marvelous marketing campaigns with their unique colour palettes and their branding assets and they were truly eye catching and they went beyond and even composed songs like "I'm Lovin' It". Their products such as Happy Meals, Big Mac, McNuggets, etc. were really able to become a regular meal for most consumers back in the days. And that was the past, and now McDonald's is experimenting with using AI for their commercials and the audience is not so happy for sure!. Specially, they even had to take down their AI generated commercial related to Christmas suddenly in last 2025 shortly after posting it on the internet and the customers were not happy at all to see "slops" in their feed.

We don't get to see creative concepts as we saw till couple of years ago. Now, it's just boring and forgettable content that you can't tell the difference apart. Further, let me attach this Reddit thread that I came across recently and they explain this in a perfect way.

Don't use AI in your marketing. If you value your brand.

Quote: "Seriously, has anyone seen a brand campaign that successfully used AI-generated content? The whole purpose of marketing is to connect with people, influence them, and foster positive perceptions. It seems people don't grasp that AI might replicate a rendering style but lacks design consistency and logical coherence. Merely looking good at first glance isn't always the best choice; using generated content could harm your hard-earned brand. What's your initial reaction upon seeing an AI-generated image on Twitter? It's often associated with fraud, cheapness, NFTs, fakeness, etc. You can almost smell that it's ChatGPT-generated copywriting, especially in images and voiceovers. Trust me, people can detect cheapness just as easily as they can spot shiny, mass-produced plastic and associate it with low quality. It's a similar feeling; people can sense it. Would you ever buy product with AI generated image? Don't think so."

There's hope still

There's a hope though. Back in 2023, we saw AI Generated videos of people eating spaghetti circulated around the internet which was often viewed as so comical and awful, but exactly after less than 3 years, we saw some real-life like videos generated by these text-to-video large language models with almost accuracy and mostly a normal user can't even tell apart what's real and what's AI generated. So, with that, here's the bitter truth. This article is written in Q3 of 2026 and AI will get better in this area and there'll be a time it can generate exactly what's in your mind (even better than that probably) and that future won't be too far considering the rapid advancements and the resource allocations we witnessed within last few years (the damage of solely relying on AI/LLMs is obviously damaging to everyone, but that's to be discussed on another day).

Till that day comes, it's your choice to decide whether you'd use AI to represent your company and if you would like to listen to my personal opinion, "please, don't!". If you are a small business or you have a limited budget, you can find thousands of very talented graphic designers out there who would do the job for few bucks. You'll be doing a help for yourself while helping out someone else as well. If even that's not affordable for you right now, switch to a platform like Canva if you don't have the knowledge or time to use graphic designing softwares. They are a proprietary platform, but it can really help you to grow your brand without a steep learning curve. Or maybe you have a hidden talent and you have a knack for learning, go for free and open source solutions like Gimp, Krita, Inkscape , etc. Also, don't forget to make a donation to them if you're satisfied with their projects. That's how open source devs who basically run the entire digital infrastructure in the world can survive at the end of the day and you can get the satisfaction that you also contributed to it somehow :).

So, please don't share AI generated images or videos in your page or for your presentations anymore (at least till it gets better).
